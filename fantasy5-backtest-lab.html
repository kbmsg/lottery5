How the test works

For every draw after an initial warm-up window, each strategy computes its 5-number pick using only draws that happened before it — it never sees the draw it's being tested against. That prediction is then checked against the real result, and the outcome is logged. This repeats across the full history, so each strategy is scored on hundreds of genuinely out-of-sample predictions, not a single lucky guess.

Why 95% confidence intervals

A hit rate from a few dozen draws can swing widely by luck alone. The Wilson interval shown under each strategy is the range its true long-run hit rate plausibly falls in, given the sample size. If that range comfortably contains the theoretical chance baseline, the strategy hasn't shown evidence of doing anything but guessing — regardless of what the headline percentage says.

On "80% accuracy" claims

Fantasy 5 drawings are independent random events with no memory of prior draws; nothing in the historical sequence carries information about the next one. Any method that reports beating chance on historical data is almost always overfit — tuned until it happens to fit the noise in that specific dataset — and that fit does not carry forward to future draws. Testing enough strategies (or enough variations of one) against the same fixed history means some will clear an arbitrary bar by chance alone; that's what this tool guards against by comparing every result to the real mathematical baseline, not to zero.
